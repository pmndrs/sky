`@pmndrs/sky` is a physically based sky, lighting and atmosphere system for
three.js on the WebGPU renderer. You drop it into a scene as
`scene.background` + `scene.environment` (image-based lighting) with one
class, and it scales up to aerial-perspective haze on distant geometry and
planet-scale (ground-to-orbit) rendering when you need it.

Its atmosphere model builds on Sébastien Hillaire's
[_A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique_](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf)
(EGSR 2020), checked against his reference code and Bruneton's precomputed
scattering. The rest is designed around three.js and frame budgets: the sky
is baked into a cube only when something changes, and the environment
lighting is re-filtered with one compute pass, so a moving sun updates
reflections in the same frame.

## Why

Most Three.js sky solutions are either a static HDRI (no time-of-day, no
atmosphere response to camera altitude) or a cheap analytic gradient
(Preetham-style — looks flat, no aerial perspective). `@pmndrs/sky` instead
bakes the real multi-scattering atmosphere integral into a small set of
lookup textures (LUTs) once per sun/atmosphere change, so you get:

- A physically-plausible sky that responds correctly to time of day,
  latitude, and camera altitude — including views from space.
- Aerial-perspective haze: distant geometry desaturates and blues out with
  distance, the way real haze does, driven by the same LUTs as the sky
  background.
- Planet-scale support: fly from the ground up through the atmosphere to
  orbit without a "seam" between two different sky implementations.
- A tiny per-frame cost — the expensive multi-scattering integration is
  baked into LUTs and only re-runs when the sun or atmosphere parameters
  change; per-frame work is a handful of cheap LUT samples plus (optionally)
  a 32×32×32 aerial-perspective compute pass.

## What you get

- **`Sky`** — the 90% facade. Construct it, `attach()` it to a scene, call
  `update(camera)` every frame. See [Your First Sky](./your-first-sky).
- **`applyHaze`** — an opt-in post-process node for aerial-perspective haze
  on your scene's opaque geometry, with optional light shafts from a
  shadow-casting sun. See the [Haze guide](../guides/haze).
- **`applyFog`** — a cheaper sky-coloured height fog with no per-frame LUT,
  for tight budgets or local weather. See
  [Height fog](../guides/haze#height-fog:-the-budget-tier).
- **A sun light** that tracks the sky (`sky.createSun()`), goes dark when the
  sun sets, and can take its brightness and colour from the atmosphere. See
  [`SkySun`](../api/sky#skysun).
- **Image-based lighting** that keeps up with the sky — `environmentTexture`
  is re-filtered with WebGPU compute on every sun or atmosphere change
  (~1 ms on the WebGPU backend). See [Baker: Environment (PMREM) refresh](<../api/baker#environment-(pmrem)-refresh>).
- **Night sky** — resolved stars and the Milky Way, placed from the same
  time, date and latitude as the sun. See [Night sky](../guides/night-sky).
- **Sky grades** — an artist-authored, time-of-day lookup table over the
  physical sky (or a plain gradient or solid colour), with a split-screen
  editor; the IBL and haze follow it. See [Sky grades](../guides/sky-grades).
- **Planet-scale support** via `planetCenter` — fly from a few metres off
  the ground to orbit with one continuous atmosphere model. See the
  [Planet-scale guide](../guides/planet-scale).
- **`SkyAtmosphereBaker`** and the individual LUT classes for power users who
  want to swap pieces or drive the pipeline directly. See the
  [API reference](../api/baker).
- **React bindings** — `<Sky>`, `<AutoHaze>` and `useSky()` for R3F. See the
  [React reference](../api/react).
- Curated atmosphere presets (`earth`, `mars`, `titan`) and full control over
  every Hillaire parameter. See [Tuning the atmosphere](../guides/tuning-atmosphere).

## Requirements

`@pmndrs/sky` targets the **WebGPU renderer** (`three/webgpu`,
`THREE.WebGPURenderer`) — see [Installation](./installation) for peer
dependency versions and WebGPU browser support notes.

## Status

Baked sky, aerial-perspective haze with light shafts, height fog,
planet-scale (ground→orbit) rendering, the night sky, and sky grades are
all functional today. Volumetric clouds are out of scope for this library.
Coming from 0.3? See [Upgrading to 0.4](../guides/upgrading).
