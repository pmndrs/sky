title: Night sky
description: Pixel-sharp stars and a Milky Way that rise with the real sky, fade in through twilight, and cost almost nothing per frame.
---

```js
import { Sky } from '@pmndrs/sky'

const sky = new Sky(renderer, { timeOfDay: 21.25, dayOfYear: 196, latitude: 38 })
sky.attach(scene)
const night = await sky.enableStars()

renderer.setAnimationLoop(() => {
  sky.update(camera) // bakes when dirty, and moves the star sprites with the camera
  renderer.render(scene, camera)
})
```

That is the whole setup. The stars and the Milky Way appear on their own as
the sun goes down and leave again at dawn — there is no day/night switch to
manage.

## What you get

The night sky is split by frequency, because a point of light and a diffuse
glow need opposite treatment:

- **Stars** are drawn as sprites in _your_ scene (`SkyStars`, added by
  `attach()`). Each is a small energy-normalised Gaussian, so it stays sharp
  at any resolution, doesn't shimmer as the camera turns, and faint stars are
  faint rather than tiny. About 9,000 stars — roughly what the eye sees on a
  dark night — with the brightest ones at their real positions.
- **The Milky Way** is a low-resolution glow map sampled by the sky shader and
  baked into the sky cube with everything else. It shows up in
  `scene.background`, in reflections, and (faintly) in the environment
  lighting.

Stars are **not** in the cube: at the cube's resolution a star smears into a
blurry blob several pixels wide.

## Driven by the sky's time and place

Both layers are oriented from the same inputs as the sun — `timeOfDay`,
`dayOfYear`, `latitude` and `north` — using local sidereal time from the same
solar model, so the stars stay registered to the sunset (to within ~0.2°):

```js
sky.setTimeOfDay(23) // the sky turns about the celestial pole
sky.setLatitude(-33.9) // southern sky
sky.setNorth('+X') // stars follow the north axis, like the sun
```

`setSunDirection()` moves only the sun; the stars keep following the time and
place you set.

Both layers are also dimmed and reddened toward the horizon by atmospheric
transmittance, hidden below the planet's horizon, and depth-tested against
your geometry, so they set behind terrain.

## Twilight

A fixed exposure has no eye adaptation, so the twilight sky renders darker
than it looks in reality and anything added on top would show far too early.
Each star, and each pixel of the Milky Way, is instead compared with the sky
directly behind it and faded in over a few stops as that sky darkens. Bright
stars appear first, the dark side of the sky fills in before the twilight
side, and the Milky Way emerges last.

`contrast` and `milkyWayContrast` move that point: higher values hold them
back until later in twilight.

## Options

`sky.enableStars(options)` (or `night.enable(options)`) updates in place;
the setters change one value without enabling a disabled night sky.

| Option              | Default | Setter                 | Effect                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `intensity`         | `1`     | `setIntensity`         | Star brightness.                                                                                 |
| `size`              | `0.7`   | `setSize`              | Star blur spot σ in CSS pixels (≈1.6 px wide at half maximum). Bright stars grow on top of this. |
| `magnitudeContrast` | `0.75`  | `setMagnitudeContrast` | `1` keeps true brightness ratios between magnitudes; lower compresses them.                      |
| `contrast`          | `40`    | `setContrast`          | Star : sky ratio at the middle of the twilight fade.                                             |
| `twinkle`           | `0.25`  | `setTwinkle`           | Scintillation depth; stronger toward the horizon.                                                |
| `count`             | `9000`  | —                      | Procedural stars (the named bright stars are extra). Rebuilds the sprites.                       |
| `seed`              | —       | —                      | Procedural field seed. Rebuilds the sprites.                                                     |
| `catalog`           | —       | —                      | Your own `StarCatalog` (see below). Rebuilds the sprites.                                        |
| `milkyWay`          | `0.25`  | `setMilkyWay`          | Milky Way intensity; `0` hides it.                                                               |
| `milkyWayContrast`  | `20`    | `setMilkyWayContrast`  | Glow : sky ratio at the middle of its twilight fade.                                             |
| `milkyWayTexture`   | —       | `setMilkyWayTexture`   | Your own map instead of the procedural one; `null` goes back.                                    |

`sky.disableStars()` hides both layers; `sky.enableStars()` brings them back.

## Your own Milky Way map or catalog

`milkyWayTexture` takes an equirectangular map in **galactic** coordinates:
the galactic centre at `u = 0.5` with longitude increasing with `u`, and
galactic latitude from −90° at `v = 0` to +90° at `v = 1`, in linear colour.
Most published all-sky maps (Gaia, for example) come in this layout; check
the licence before shipping one. A texture you pass in is never disposed by
the sky. `generateMilkyWayTexture({ width })` regenerates the procedural one
at another resolution.

`catalog` takes `{ directions, magnitudes, tones, count }`: unit
**equatorial** direction vectors (`x = cos δ cos α`, `y = sin δ`,
`z = cos δ sin α`), visual magnitudes, and a 0 (warm) to 1 (hot) colour per
star. `generateStarCatalog()` shows the expected shape.

## Cost

Measured on an M5 Pro, per frame, over the plain cube background:

|                              | 1920×1080 | 3840×2160    |
| ---------------------------- | --------- | ------------ |
| Stars + Milky Way (9k stars) | ~0.01 ms  | ~0.05–0.2 ms |
| 50k stars                    | ~0.05 ms  | ~0.05 ms     |
| Daytime (stars invisible)    | ~0        | ~0           |

The Milky Way lives in the cube, so it only costs anything when the cube
re-bakes. Moving the time of day re-bakes the cube anyway — that is the sun
moving — and dominates everything above: see
`examples/vanilla/16-stars-bench.html` and `scripts/bench-stars.mjs`.

## Upgrading from 0.3

The procedural hash-grid starfield and the HDRI star map were baked into the
cube, which is what made them blurry, and are gone:

| Before                                 | Now                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `enableStars({ source: 'hdri', url })` | Load the map yourself (it must be in galactic coordinates) and pass `milkyWayTexture`. Point stars in an HDRI blur in the cube; use the sprites. |
| `density`, `setStarsDensity`           | `count`                                                                                                                                          |
| `brightness`, `setStarsBrightness`     | `intensity` / `setStarsIntensity`                                                                                                                |
| `rotation`, `setStarsRotation`         | Follows `timeOfDay`, `dayOfYear`, `latitude` and `north` automatically.                                                                          |
| `setStarsSource`                       | —                                                                                                                                                |
